Candidate 98: Sulci Gordii, east of Olympus Mons

Weak · roughly 35%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 35 m across, with a short faint tail to the south, appeared on lightly ridged plains in Sulci Gordii, 317 km east of the summit of Olympus Mons, between October 2020 and April 2025, in one CTX image so far.

Why it may be a new impact. Two earlier images, of July and October 2020, show no dark spot there, and the spot shows up as a change in the aligned before-and-after difference; a compact spot with a dark core that was not there before is how the blast zone of a small new crater can look.

Location
17.906° N, 231.773° E (128.227° W)
Absent
25 July 2020 and 25 October 2020
Present
4 April 2025
Size
Compact dark spot about 35 m across (28 × 40 m), a dark core with a short faint tail to the south
Confidence
Weak candidate; roughly 35% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
It is seen in only one heavily compressed image and spans only about 5 × 7 CTX pixels, so it may be noise or a compression artefact, and since both earlier images were taken under a lower sun from another direction, it may be shading or a brightness difference seen only under the April 2025 sun. The earlier images are also strongly striped and poorly aligned, the nearby feature used as a check is faint, and a dust devil that stalled is possible too. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 9.5 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
