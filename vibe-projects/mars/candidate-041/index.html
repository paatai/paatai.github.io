Candidate 41: Acheron Fossae region, beside a small crater

Strong · roughly 80%

What changed. A compact dark dot with a core about 50 m across appeared 70 m west of an old small crater in the Acheron Fossae region, between January 2012 and April 2023. It is in three CTX images from three orbits, from April 2023 to January 2025.

Why it may be a new impact. An image of 8 January 2012, taken under the same sun as the April 2023 image, shows the old crater but no dot, so the dot is not the shadow of a crater that was already there. A dark feature 170 m to the south-west shows clearly in that image, so it would have shown the dot. A compact dark dot that appears and lasts is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
33.290° N, 230.732° E (129.268° W)
Absent
8 January 2012
Present
12 April 2023, 11 June 2023 and 28 January 2025
Size
Compact dark dot with a core about 50 m across
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 80% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one earlier image shows the ground without it, so it could have formed at any time in an 11-year window. Darker ground exposed by wind is unlikely for so compact a dot but is not ruled out. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 20.9 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
