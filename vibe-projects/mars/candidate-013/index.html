Candidate 13: Plains south of Acheron Fossae

Strong · roughly 70%

What changed. A diffuse dark patch 100 to 120 m across, with a darker core, appeared on textured plains south of Acheron Fossae between May 2016 and September 2025. It looks the same in two CTX images from September and October 2025.

Why it may be a new impact. Three earlier images, from 2007, 2009 and 2016, show nothing there, although an older feature 300 m away shows in each; the May 2016 one was taken under the same sun as the September 2025 image. In October 2025 the patch looks the same under a different sun and viewing angle. Dark rather than bright, and seen in northern summer, it is not frost, and a round dark patch with a darker core fits the blast zone of a small new crater.

Location
35.512° N, 230.446° E (129.554° W)
Absent
23 March 2007, 25 August 2009 and 11 May 2016
Present
1 September 2025 and 26 October 2025
Size
Diffuse dark patch 100 to 120 m across, with a darker core
Confidence
Strong candidate: roughly 70% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
It could have formed at any time in the nine years between May 2016 and September 2025. A slope streak, a dark streak left by dust sliding down a slope, is unlikely: the patch is round and there is no suitable slope. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 15.2 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
