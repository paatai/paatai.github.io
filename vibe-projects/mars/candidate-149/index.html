Candidate 149: Wind-etched plains east of Medusae Fossae, 215 km north-west of Minio Vallis

Strong · roughly 85%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 30 m across appeared on smooth wind-etched plains east of Medusae Fossae, 215 km north-west of Minio Vallis, between February 2008 and February 2023 and is still there in October 2024, in three CTX images from three orbits taken at three viewing angles, at the same size in all three.

Why it may be a new impact. The only earlier image, from 2008, taken under almost the same sun as the February 2023 image, shows plain ground there, and a compact dark spot that stays in place for 20 months is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
1.308° S, 206.388° E (153.612° W)
Absent
23 February 2008
Present
9 February 2023, 1 April 2023 and 21 October 2024
Size
Dark spot about 30 m across, elongated north-south (24 × 42 m in the February 2023 image, 26 × 44 m in the April 2023 image and 24 × 42 m in the October 2024 image)
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 85% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one earlier image exists, so the formation window spans 15 years and rests on it alone, and the plains have no strong dark feature nearby that could show whether that image would have caught a spot like it, although that image lines up well and is sharp. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 2.6 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
