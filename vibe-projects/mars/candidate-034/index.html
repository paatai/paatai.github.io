Candidate 34: Elysium Planitia, platy lava plains west of Athabasca Valles

Strong · roughly 85%

What changed. A dark core 25 to 30 m across, inside a diffuse halo about 100 m across, appeared on platy lava plains west of Athabasca Valles between August 2008 and June 2021. It is in five CTX images from five orbits up to May 2026, very dark until 2025 and fainter in 2026.

Why it may be a new impact. The August 2008 image, taken under the same sun as the June 2021 one, lines up well and shows nothing at the spot, while a dark feature 226 m to the south-east shows in it, so it would have shown the spot. In the five later images the sun shines from directions up to 65° apart, so it is not a shadow, and a round core-and-halo spot that keeps its shape for five years is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
7.001° N, 150.271° E
Absent

Present
8 June 2021, 7 July 2024, 6 January 2025, 13 July 2025 and 3 May 2026
Size

Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 85% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one earlier image, from August 2008, shows the ground without it, so it could have formed at any time in the nearly 13 years before June 2021. Darker ground exposed by wind is the remaining alternative, but it is unlikely: the spot is round, has no streak and has kept the same shape for five years. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 14.3 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
