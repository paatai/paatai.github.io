Candidate 56: Plains north-west of Echus Chasma

Strong · roughly 90%

What changed. A compact dark core about 55 × 45 m, inside a soft halo, appeared among small knobs north-west of Echus Chasma, between May 2008 and January 2025, and is still there in May 2025, in two CTX images from two orbits.

Why it may be a new impact. A May 2008 image, taken under the same sun as the May 2025 image and with every knob lined up, shows nothing there, so the spot is not a shadow, while a dark feature 300 m to the north-east shows clearly in it. A compact dark core with a halo is the classic look of a small fresh impact.

Location

Size

Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 90% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
It has been seen for only three and a half months, so darker ground exposed by the wind is not ruled out, although a compact core with a halo makes that unlikely. With only one earlier image, the window is nearly 17 years long. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 5.5 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
