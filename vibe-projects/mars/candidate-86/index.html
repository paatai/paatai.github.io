Candidate 86: Wind-streaked plains on south-eastern Amazonis Mensa, 113 km south-west of Amazonis Sulci

Strong · roughly 90%

What changed. A dark blast zone appeared on wind-streaked plains on south-eastern Amazonis Mensa, 113 km south-west of Amazonis Sulci, between September 2008 and January 2022: a dark core about 150 m across with short dark arms and a chain of smaller dark blobs to the east, inside a diffuse dark halo about 650 m across, in two CTX images from two orbits ten months apart.

Why it may be a new impact. The only earlier image, from 2008, shows plain ground there under nearly the same sun as the first image with the spot, and a dark core with arms and a halo appearing on dusty plains is how the blast zone of a new impact looks.

Location
2.981° S, 214.577° E (145.423° W)
Absent
1 September 2008
Present
18 January 2022 and 12 November 2022
Size
Dark core about 150 m across, inside a halo about 650 m across
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 90% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
There is only one earlier image, so the window is thirteen years long; no comparable dark feature lies nearby to test the earlier image; and the second image is poorly aligned, although the spot is plain in it. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
