Candidate 34: Central Ulysses Fossae, Tharsis lava plains

Strong · roughly 90%

What changed. A compact, very dark spot about 45 m across appeared on smooth lava plains beside a graben (a long fault trough) in central Ulysses Fossae, between April 2010 and October 2022. It is unchanged in three later images, up to May 2025, on four orbits in all.

Why it may be a new impact. An April 2010 image, taken under the same sun as the May 2025 one, would have shown it clearly and shows nothing. Later images, with the sun in many directions, show the same spot, so it is not a shadow; it is on flat ground, not on the graben’s wall, so it is not a slope streak; and two and a half years without change argue against a short-lived patch made by wind.

Caveat
Only one earlier image exists, so the window is twelve years long. It first shows in a 2022 image, so the camera teams may already have it on their unpublished lists; but it lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera, and the teams normally photograph a new impact with HiRISE within months of noticing it. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
