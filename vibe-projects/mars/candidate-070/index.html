Candidate 70: Tharsis plains 490 km south of Ascraeus Mons

Promising · roughly 60%

What changed. A compact, very dark spot about 19 m across appeared on the Tharsis lava plains 490 km south of Ascraeus Mons, between April 2024 and March 2025, in one CTX image so far.

Why it may be a new impact. Four earlier images, from 2012 to April 2024, show plain ground there, one of them, from February 2012, under the same sun as the March 2025 image, while a dark feature 280 m to the east shows in the first three. The spot is a smooth blob about four pixels across rather than a blocky compression flaw, and a compact, very dark new spot is how the blast zone of a small new crater can look.

Location
3.871° N, 257.693° E (102.307° W)
Absent
20 February 2012, 15 September 2012, 19 June 2022 and 8 April 2024
Present
29 March 2025
Size
Compact, very dark spot about 19 m across (16 × 22 m)
Confidence
Promising candidate; roughly 60% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one image shows it so far, and that image is heavily compressed, so a compression flaw or a dust devil caught in the act is possible; only a second image can rule out a short-lived mark. The April 2024 image lines up poorly, and without it the window would reach back to June 2022. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 6.9 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
