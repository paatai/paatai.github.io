Candidate 33: Western Arabia Terra, about 700 km west of Pasteur crater

Strong · roughly 95%

What changed. A dark patch about 40 m across appeared on the north-west rim of an older crater 80 m wide in western Arabia Terra, between March 2014 and May 2023. It is still there in three images from January to March 2025. A fainter dark band that runs about 70 m south from it, along the old crater’s rim, is partly new too, perhaps a small dust avalanche set off by the impact.

Why it may be a new impact. Images from 2010 and 2014 show nothing there, and both would have shown it; the 2014 image was taken under nearly the same sun as the 2023 one, so the patch is not a shadow. By chance, NASA’s HiRISE camera photographed the spot in March 2025, on the same orbit as a CTX image. It shows a pit about 2.5 m wide at the centre of the dark patch, shaded like a small bowl (dark on the side facing the sun, bright on the far side): just what a small fresh impact leaves.

Location
19.143° N, 12.188° E
Absent
17 February 2010 and 18 March 2014
Present
4 May 2023, 6 January 2025, 3 February 2025 and 25 March 2025 (CTX and HiRISE)
Size
Dark patch about 40 m across, with a pit about 2.5 m wide at its centre
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 95% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
The HiRISE image was aimed at the surrounding terrain, not at the spot, but the HiRISE team may have noticed it. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
