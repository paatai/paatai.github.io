Candidate 19: Giant lobe north of Pavonis Mons

Strong · roughly 85%

What changed. A compact dark spot 25 to 30 m across appeared on the giant lobe north of Pavonis Mons between September 2024 and March 2025, a window of 165 days. Four CTX images from four orbits, up to January 2026, show it slowly fading.

Why it may be a new impact. Six earlier images, from September 2019 to September 2024, show nothing there, while a dark feature 250 m away shows in each, so they would have shown the spot. It is missing at every sun angle in the images up to 2024 and present at every sun angle since, so it is not a shadow, and a compact spot that slowly fades is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
4.734° N, 246.654° E (113.346° W)
Absent
23 September 2019, 25 July 2021, 8 August 2023, 12 December 2023, 23 April 2024 and 26 September 2024
Present
10 March 2025, 10 May 2025, 15 September 2025 and 31 January 2026
Size
Compact dark spot 25 to 30 m across
Confidence
Strong candidate: roughly 85% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
Shadows and camera flaws are both ruled out; what it still lacks is a view at higher resolution. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 0.6 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
