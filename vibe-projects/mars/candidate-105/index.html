Candidate 105: Plains 276 km north-east of Tharsis Tholus

Strong · roughly 70%

What changed. A diffuse dark spot about 70 m across, with a darker core and elongated north–south, appeared on lava plains 276 km north-east of Tharsis Tholus, between February 2010 and June 2020, and is still there, unfaded, in February 2025, in two CTX images from two orbits.

Why it may be a new impact. An image of February 2010, under almost the same sun as the February 2025 image, shows no spot there, while a dark feature 260 m to the west-south-west shows in it; the aligned before-and-after difference is deepest at the spot except at the edge of the 2 × 2 km area around it, and a dark spot with a darker core that has not faded over the 4.7 years between its two images is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
15.544° N, 273.495° E (86.505° W)
Absent
19 February 2010
Present
25 June 2020 and 22 February 2025
Size
Diffuse dark spot about 69 m across (58 × 114 m), a darker core in a diffuse zone elongated north–south
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 70% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one usable earlier image exists, and a dark band that was already there, 100 to 150 m to the south-west, runs the same way as the spot’s long axis, so the spot may be a newly darkened stretch of it, from dust sliding in a shallow fissure or wind scouring along it. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 40.8 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
