Candidate 9: Twin dark spot on the plains of Elysium Planitia

Promising · roughly 75%

What changed. Two compact dark spots, each about 20 to 25 m across and 35 m apart, appeared on the smooth plains of Elysium Planitia between January 2008 and August 2024, and look identical in three CTX images from August 2024 to December 2025.

Why it may be a new impact. All three earlier images, from 2006 to 2008, show nothing there, while an older dark spot 190 m away shows in each of them and lines up in every image, so they would have caught the pair. Two compact spots that stay unchanged for 16 months fit the blast zones of small new impacts better than dark ground uncovered by wind.

Location
6.089° N, 157.220° E
Absent
3 October 2006, 4 December 2006 and 27 January 2008
Present
11 August 2024, 22 December 2024 and 16 December 2025
Size
Two dark spots, each about 20 to 25 m across, 35 m apart, one north of the other
Confidence
Promising candidate; roughly 75% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
It is rated promising rather than strong only because none of the earlier images lines up with the later ones as closely as the search’s rule requires, as the plains are almost featureless. No CTX image of the site was taken between January 2008 and August 2024, so it could have formed at any time in that gap of more than 16 years. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 37 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
