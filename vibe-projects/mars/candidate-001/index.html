Candidate 1: Arsia Sulci

Promising · roughly 75%

What changed. A round dark spot about 70 m across, inside a fainter halo about 150 m across, appeared in Arsia Sulci between October 2022 and April 2026. So far it is seen in only one CTX image.

Why it may be a new impact. Four earlier images, aligned with the April 2026 one, show nothing at the spot, not even a landform. They were taken with the sun both higher and lower in the sky than in April 2026, so the spot is not a shadow. A round dark spot with a soft halo is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Confidence
Promising candidate: roughly 75% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
Only one image shows it so far, and a flaw in that single image cannot be fully ruled out. A small slope streak, a dark streak left by dust sliding down a slope, is unlikely, as the spot is round. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 12 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
