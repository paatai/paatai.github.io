Candidate 82: South-eastern Ulysses Fossae

Promising · roughly 65%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 40 m across appeared on flat lava plains about 150 m west of the foot of a ridge in south-eastern Ulysses Fossae, between May 2024 and December 2024, in one CTX image so far.

Why it may be a new impact. Three earlier images, from 2010, 2022 and May 2024, line up well and show no spot there, while knobs and small dark marks around the site show in every image; the last was taken only 222 days before the image with the spot. The spot has a diffuse margin and sits on the ground texture, which makes a flaw in the image less likely, and a compact new dark spot like this could be the blast zone of a small new crater.

Location
8.324° N, 239.294° E (120.706° W)
Absent
12 March 2010, 19 July 2022 and 13 May 2024
Present
21 December 2024
Size
Compact dark spot about 40 m across (34 × 44 m)
Confidence
Promising candidate; roughly 65% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one image shows it so far, so an image defect or a dust devil that stalled cannot be fully excluded. Dark material shed from the ridge 150 m away is also possible, though no streak links the two. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 14.4 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
