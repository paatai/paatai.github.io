Candidate 166: Lava plains 350 km south-east of Fesenkov crater, 412 km north-east of Tharsis Tholus

Strong · roughly 85%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 40 m across appeared on lava plains south-east of Fesenkov crater between May 2020 and June 2022. Two CTX images from two orbits, 31 months apart, show it.

Why it may be a new impact. The two earlier images, from 2019 and 2020, show plain ground there, one of them under nearly the same sun as the first image with the spot. A dark feature 200 m away shows in both, so they would have shown a new spot too. A compact dark spot that appears on dusty plains and lasts is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
16.198° N, 275.824° E (84.176° W)
Absent
5 March 2019 and 30 May 2020
Present
12 June 2022 and 4 January 2025
Size
Dark spot about 40 m across (46 × 38 m in the June 2022 image)
Confidence
Strong candidate: roughly 85% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
Only two images show it. When the aligned May 2020 image is subtracted from the June 2022 one, both taken under nearly the same sun, a place near a small crater 790 m away, whose shading varies with the direction of the sun, shows about as much change as the spot. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 19.8 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
