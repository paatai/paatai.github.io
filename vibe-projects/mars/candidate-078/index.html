Candidate 78: Lava plains north of Ascraeus Mons

Strong · roughly 80%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 70 m across appeared on knobby lava plains 250 km north of the summit of Ascraeus Mons between February 2008 and February 2021, and shows in two CTX images from two orbits. In 2021 a faint dark streak ran about 300 m north-west from it; by January 2025 the streak was gone and the spot was fading.

Why it may be a new impact. The only earlier image, from February 2008, was taken under the same sun as the January 2025 one; it shows knob shadows near the site but nothing like the spot, and a dark mark 250 m to the west does show in it. Four years later the streak had gone, as a ray of a fresh blast zone would, but the spot was still clear, longer than dust-devil marks usually last.

Location
16.071° N, 256.461° E (103.539° W)
Absent
28 February 2008
Present
12 February 2021 and 11 January 2025
Size
Compact dark spot about 70 m across (88 × 76 m in the February 2021 image), fading
Confidence
Strong candidate: roughly 80% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
Only one earlier image exists, so the spot could have formed at any time in a 13-year span. The 2021 streak could be the track of a dust devil that stalled, and dust stripped from a patch by wind is also possible. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 28.9 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
