Candidate 101: Gordii Fossae

Promising · roughly 60%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 25 m across appeared on ridged plains in Gordii Fossae, between January 2023 and December 2024, in one CTX image so far.

Why it may be a new impact. Five earlier images from 2008 to 2023, all aligned, show no spot there, two of them, of January 2008 and January 2023, under the same sun as the December 2024 image, while a dark feature 216 m to the west shows clearly in the January 2008 image; the spot is far darker than the striping of the image, and a compact new dark spot is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
16.498° N, 233.921° E (126.079° W)
Absent
21 January 2008, 19 February 2016, 24 February 2016, 14 October 2022 and 30 January 2023
Present
4 December 2024
Size
Compact dark spot about 25 m across (28 × 24 m)
Confidence
Promising candidate; roughly 60% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one image shows it, so an image defect or a cluster of noise cannot be fully ruled out, though the spot spans about 5 × 4 CTX pixels. In the aligned before-and-after difference a change at a corner of the 2 × 2 km area is deeper than the spot, and the nearby feature used as a check shows clearly only in the January 2008 image. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera; the nearest ends 10.0 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
