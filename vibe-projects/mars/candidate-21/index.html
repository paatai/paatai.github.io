Candidate 21: Eastern Ulysses Fossae, Tharsis lava plains

Strong · roughly 90%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 70 by 55 m appeared on smooth lava plains in eastern Ulysses Fossae, between March 2019 and March 2024. It has not changed in five images from five orbits, up to July 2025.

Why it may be a new impact. Images from January 2010 and March 2019 show nothing there, and both would have shown it. There is no pit, knob or crater at the spot to cast a shadow, the ground is flat, so it is not a slope streak, and it is not a track. A new impact scar is the natural explanation.

Location
14.229° N, 238.270° E (121.730° W)
Absent
27 January 2010 and 19 March 2019
Present
five images from 19 March 2024 to 9 July 2025
Size
Compact dark spot about 70 × 55 m
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 90% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
