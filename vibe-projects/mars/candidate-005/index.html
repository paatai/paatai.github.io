Candidate 5: Cerberus plains

Strong · roughly 70%

What changed. A rayed dark spot 100 to 150 m across, with dark fingers reaching out from a core of several dark blobs, appeared on the flat lava plains of Cerberus between October 2010 and October 2023. It looks identical in four CTX images taken over two years.

Why it may be a new impact. The only earlier image, from October 2010, was taken with the sun at almost the same height as in the four later images, from October 2023 to November 2025, and shows nothing there. Dark fingers spreading out from a dark core are how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
3.723° N, 166.755° E
Absent
10 October 2010
Present
8 October 2023, 7 December 2023, 4 January 2024 and 14 November 2025
Size
Rayed dark spot 100 to 150 m across
Confidence
Strong candidate: roughly 70% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
Wind stripping dust from a patch of ground could also make such a mark, while a slope streak (a dark streak left by dust sliding down a slope) on the narrow fracture beside it is unlikely. The spot might already be in a catalogue of new impacts from the era of NASA’s InSight lander (Bickel et al. 2025) that could not be downloaded, though that is unlikely: every entry there has a HiRISE image, and none covers this spot. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 39 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
