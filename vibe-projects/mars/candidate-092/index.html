Candidate 92: Western Acheron Fossae

Promising · roughly 65%

What changed. A compact dark spot about 65 m across, with a darker core, appeared on smooth ground on the floor of a trough in western Acheron Fossae between 13 April and 3 June 2025, a window of only 51 days. It is visible in one CTX image so far.

Why it may be a new impact. Seven earlier images, from 2008 to 2025, show no spot there, including two of 12 March and 13 April 2025 taken under the same sun as the June 2025 image. When an earlier image is subtracted from the later one, the strongest change in the surrounding 2 × 2 km is right at the spot and nowhere else. A round dark spot with a darker core and a gradually fading edge, appearing within about seven weeks, is how the blast zone of a small new crater looks.

Location
38.530° N, 220.808° E (139.192° W)
Absent
18 June 2008, 4 July 2016, 2 September 2016, 1 April 2018, 17 April 2018, 12 March 2025 and 13 April 2025
Present
3 June 2025
Size
Compact dark spot about 65 m across (76 × 66 m), a darker core with a lighter patch on its west side
Confidence
Promising candidate: roughly 65% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
Only one image shows it, though the spot is round with a gradually fading edge and that image has no line defect near it. No comparably dark feature nearby can confirm that the earlier images would have shown it. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 1.7 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
