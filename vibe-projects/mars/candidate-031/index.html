Candidate 31: Eastern Ulysses Fossae, Tharsis lava plains

Strong · roughly 80%

What changed. A compact dark spot 70 × 55 m appeared on smooth Tharsis lava plains between north-south fault troughs in eastern Ulysses Fossae, between March 2019 and March 2024. It has not changed in five CTX images from five orbits, up to July 2025.

Why it may be a new impact. An image of 27 January 2010, which lines up well and was taken with the sun at almost the same height as in the 2 January 2025 image, shows no spot and no landform there, while a dark feature 192 m to the west shows. The five images with the spot have the sun from directions spanning 47°, so it is not a shadow, and a compact spot that stays unchanged fits a small new impact.

Location
14.227° N, 238.274° E (121.726° W)
Absent
27 January 2010 and 19 March 2019
Present
19 March 2024, 2 January 2025, 17 April 2025, 11 June 2025 and 9 July 2025
Size
Compact dark spot 70 × 55 m
Confidence
Strong candidate: roughly 80% likely to be a new impact.
Caveat
The March 2019 image does not show the dark feature used as a check, so the firm window runs from January 2010 to March 2024. Wind could have exposed darker ground around a tiny pit, but that is unlikely: the well-aligned 2010 image shows no landform there, and the spot has stayed compact and unchanged for 16 months. No image from the high-resolution HiRISE camera covers it; the nearest ends 10.2 km away. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
