Candidate 41: Tharsis lava plains 350 km west of Echus Chasma

Strong · roughly 85%

What changed. A compact, very dark spot 35 to 45 m across appeared on smooth lava plains in Tharsis, 350 km west of Echus Chasma, between January 2010 and January 2023. A much smaller dark dot about 70 m to its north-west is also new in the 2023 image. The spot is still there in April and July 2025.

Why it may be a new impact. The only earlier image, from January 2010, was taken under a similar sun and would have shown the spot, but shows nothing there. The spot is in three images from three orbits over nearly two and a half years, so it is not a shadow, a camera glitch or a short-lived mark left by a dust devil, and the lava plains are smooth, so it is not a slope streak. A compact, very dark spot like this is the typical mark of a small new impact.

Location
2.437° N, 274.057° E (85.943° W)
Absent
23 January 2010
Present
27 January 2023, 12 April 2025 and 5 July 2025
Size
Compact, very dark spot 35 to 45 m across
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 85% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
Only one earlier image exists, so the window is 13 years long. It first shows in a 2023 image, so the camera teams may already have it on their unpublished lists; but it lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera, and the teams normally photograph a new impact with HiRISE within months of noticing it. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
