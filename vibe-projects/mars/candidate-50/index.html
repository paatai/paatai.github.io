Candidate 50: Plains in south-eastern Gordii Fossae

Strong · roughly 75%

What changed. A dark spot with a head about 60 m across and a tail about 140 m long appeared on plains in south-eastern Gordii Fossae by June 2016 and is still there, fading, in March 2024, in three CTX images from three orbits.

Why it may be a new impact. The only earlier image, from 2007, taken under almost the same sun as the 2024 one, shows plain ground there. A sudden dark spot with a short tail fits the blast zone of a small new impact.

Location
13.636° N, 234.030° E (125.970° W)
Absent
6 May 2007
Present
8 June 2016, 29 March 2019 and 29 March 2024
Size
Dark spot with a head about 60 m across and a tail about 140 m long, fading
Confidence
Strong candidate; roughly 75% that it is a new impact.
Caveat
The spot is faint and fading, no dark feature nearby could test whether the 2007 image would have shown one, and the aligned before-and-after difference at the spot is weak, so it is rated about 75% rather than 90%. It lies outside every footprint of the high-resolution HiRISE camera. Like every candidate here, it is unconfirmed: see the caveat.
