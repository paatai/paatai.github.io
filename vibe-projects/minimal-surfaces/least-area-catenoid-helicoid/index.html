Small patches have least area: Enneper, catenoid, helicoid (no voice)

Silent animation

Three panels, all exact minimal surfaces: Enneper’s surface, the catenoid and the helicoid. On each, a small round patch is pushed in and out in several ways that keep its boundary curve fixed; every pushed version has larger area, and the counters show the area going up. This is the defining property: every small enough patch of a minimal surface has the least area among all surfaces with the same boundary.

The last part shows why “small enough” matters. A soap film between two equal rings a distance h apart: when the rings are close, there are two catenoids (a fat one and a thin one); only the fat one is stable, and the thin one, although it is a minimal surface, does not have least area. Pull the rings farther apart than about 1.3255 times the radius and no catenoid joins them at all; the film snaps into two flat disks.
