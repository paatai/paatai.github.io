Galaxy hunt

An automated search of public sky surveys, including Gaia DR3, looked for stars that changed brightness in ways the published record does not already list. These pages show twelve candidates from that search. Every probability is the search’s own estimate, not reviewed by an outside expert, and nothing has been reported or submitted anywhere. A probability is the chance that the candidate is a real, previously unrecorded event of the kind claimed. On the light curves, down means fainter. Candidate 1 also has difference images, and there a blue spot means fainter.

Unexplained changes

  • Candidate 1: red dwarf in Phoenix that faded by up to 38% for about a year from November 2016, then dipped again briefly (10%) in November 2018 Gaia DR3 6521694778536448640 · about 60%
  • Candidate 2: white dwarf in Cepheus that faded by 26% for about eight months in 2015. Only Gaia was watching Gaia DR3 2212797391765189760 · about 35%
  • Candidate 3: orange dwarf in Aquila, steady from 2009 to 2013, then fading since 2014, and still fading in 2025 Gaia DR3 4296242940002232704 · about 35%
  • Candidate 4: orange dwarf in front of the Andromeda Galaxy, steady for decades, then 60–90% brighter from 2010 to 2020. It spins fast, so a giant starspot cycle is now likelier than dust Gaia DR3 381248613189061632 · about 30%
  • Candidate 5: orange dwarf near the Omega Nebula that faded from 2010 to 2014, then brightened by about half a magnitude until 2022 Gaia DR3 4096419827121636736 · about 25%
  • Candidate 6: orange dwarf in Aquarius, fainter on plates from 1951 to 1994 than it is today. It spins every 3.9 days, so a long starspot cycle is the likelier cause Gaia DR3 6908199778856910464 · about 15%

Possible new microlensing events

A microlensing event is an expected kind of event, not an anomaly. Here the probability measures only whether each one is real and previously unrecorded.

  • Candidate 7: distant star in Serpens, toward the inner Galaxy, that brightened by 60% for about two months in 2016, once Gaia DR3 4145977633638478336 · about 60%
  • Candidate 8: distant star in Puppis, roughly opposite the Galactic centre, that brightened by 70% over a few months in early 2015, once Gaia DR3 5620272814897694848 · about 55%
  • Candidate 9: star in Sagittarius, toward the Galactic bulge, that brightened at least two-fold around the turn of 2014/2015, once Gaia DR3 4119926316171065984 · about 50%
  • Candidate 10: faint, distant star in Cygnus that brightened four-fold for a few weeks in 2016, once, by the same factor in every colour Gaia DR3 1861208444041823360 · about 45%

Possible hidden companions

  • Candidate 11: star in Ursa Major whose Gaia wobble suggests an unseen companion of about 1.5 solar masses Gaia DR3 1121443762310292096 · about 10%
  • Candidate 12: giant star in Vulpecula whose Gaia wobble suggests an unseen companion of about 4.3 solar masses Gaia DR3 2018177545831527808 · about 5%
