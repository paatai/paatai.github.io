Galaxy hunt

Public sky surveys, including Gaia DR3, have caught twelve stars behaving in ways that the published record does not already list. Six faded or brightened without a clear cause. Four brightened once and briefly, as a distant star does when the gravity of an object passing in front of it focuses its light. Two wobble on the sky as if pulled by an unseen companion. Stars like these can reveal dust where none is expected, a foreground star or dark object acting as a gravitational lens, or a heavy companion that gives off no light. All twelve are unconfirmed candidates. The percentage beside each one is an estimate of the chance that it is a real, previously unrecorded case of the kind described, and none of these estimates has been checked by outside experts. On the light curves, down means fainter. Candidate 1 also has difference images, and on those a blue spot means fainter.

Possible new microlensing events

A microlensing event is an expected kind of event, not an anomaly. When a foreground star or a dark object passes almost exactly in front of a distant star, its gravity focuses the distant star’s light, which brightens and fades once, smoothly and by the same factor in every colour. For these four stars, the percentage measures only the chance that the event is real and has not been recorded before.

  • Candidate 7: a distant star in Serpens, towards the inner Galaxy, that brightened once, by 60%, for about two months in 2016 Gaia DR3 4145977633638478336 · about 60%
  • Candidate 8: a distant star in Puppis, roughly opposite the Galactic centre, that brightened once, by 70%, over a few months in early 2015 Gaia DR3 5620272814897694848 · about 55%
  • Candidate 9: a star in Sagittarius, towards the Galactic bulge, that brightened once, at least two-fold, around the turn of 2014/2015 Gaia DR3 4119926316171065984 · about 50%
  • Candidate 10: a faint, distant star in Cygnus that brightened once, four-fold and by the same factor in every colour, for a few weeks in 2016 Gaia DR3 1861208444041823360 · about 45%

Possible hidden companions

  • Candidate 11: a star in Ursa Major whose wobble on the sky, measured by Gaia, suggests an unseen companion of about 1.5 solar masses Gaia DR3 1121443762310292096 · about 10%
  • Candidate 12: a giant star in Vulpecula whose wobble on the sky, measured by Gaia, suggests an unseen companion of about 4.3 solar masses Gaia DR3 2018177545831527808 · about 5%
