Candidate 38: an old Sun-like star that was slightly faint for two years

about 35%

Why this estimate. The dimming is real: ZTF recorded it on two of its detectors and ASAS-SN, a separate network of small telescopes, saw it in all five of its cameras that watched the star. Green light dimmed more than red, and the infrared did not change, which rules out a solid body. But the dip is shallow, and spots or another slow cause are harder to exclude for a change this small.

How to read the pictures. The finder chart shows a patch of sky two arcminutes across, about a fifteenth of the Moon’s width, with north up and east to the left. The star sits between the two yellow ticks, and the white bar is 30 arcseconds long. On a light curve the vertical axis is magnitude, a brightness scale on which larger numbers mean fainter stars, and it is drawn so that down means fainter. Purple shading marks the dimming. Each survey uses slightly different filters, so each is compared with its own normal level. The ZTF pictures show ZTF’s reference image of the star, an image taken during the dimming, and the difference between the two, in which only what changed remains: blue means fainter and red brighter. On the TESS light curves the star is black and the comparison stars are coloured.

What changed. The star is a Sun-like star, about 5,700 K and some 10 billion years old, that is beginning to leave the main sequence, about 920 parsecs away in Cepheus. ZTF saw it steady from 2018 to late 2021. From early 2022 it was about 0.04 magnitudes fainter in green light, and in July to September 2023 0.07 in green and 0.03 in red, about 7 per cent less light. It was close to normal again during 2024. ZTF’s alert system flagged it 17 times, and ASAS-SN saw the same episode.

Why it might matter. Blue light dimmed about 1.7 times as much as red light, more than starspots usually give (1.1 to 1.3) and at least as much as ordinary interstellar dust. WISE, observing every six months, saw no clear change at 3.4 microns, so whatever dimmed the star blocked visible light more than infrared light, as dust does. TESS found no rotation in any of seven sectors, so the star does not look spotted. A thin dust cloud drifting slowly across an old star would fit.

No record of the dimming could be found. The star is not in SIMBAD, VSX (the variable-star index), Gaia’s variable-star lists or the other catalogues consulted, nor among the dimming stars of Tzanidakis et al. (2025), and there is no Transient Name Server entry, Gaia alert or paper giving its Gaia number. ZTF’s alert system holds only machine-generated classifications for it.

Caveat
The dimming is shallow, only a few per cent for most of its length. The colour ratio differs between the two ZTF fields (1.5 and 2.0), and GALEX gives only a mild hint of ultraviolet light. There is no spectrum.
