Candidate 4: a giant star with a possibly heavy dark companion

Weak

How to read the pictures. The finder chart is a patch of sky two arcminutes across, about a fifteenth of the Moon’s width, with north up and east to the left. The star sits between the two yellow ticks, and the white bar is 30 arcseconds. On a light curve the vertical axis is magnitude. A larger magnitude means a fainter star, so the axis runs downwards: down means fainter. Orange shading marks the change. Surveys use slightly different filters, so each one is compared with its own normal level.

What the search found. A cool giant star in Vulpecula, in the plane of the Milky Way, at about 4,600 K. It is dimmed by about 2.4 magnitudes of interstellar dust. Gaia’s orbit on the sky has a period of 1,032 days and is quite eccentric. If the giant has the 3.5 solar masses Gaia estimates, the companion has 4.3 solar masses, with a range of 3.7 to 5.9. A normal star of 4.3 solar masses would outshine the giant, so the companion would have to be dark. The light curve is steady where it can be trusted. The candidate is the wobble, not a change in brightness.

Why the rating is weak. The orbital period is as long as the whole Gaia DR3 data set, 1,038 days, so the size and shape of the orbit are poorly pinned down. The conclusion also depends on an uncertain mass for the giant. At about 6 solar masses the companion could be an ordinary star.
