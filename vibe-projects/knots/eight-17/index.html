8₁₇

Watch the video (MP4, 4 MB)

Silent animation

Why it is remarkable. Give a knot a direction, marked by arrows. The knot is invertible if it can be moved onto itself with every arrow reversed. 8₁₇ is the smallest knot that is not invertible. Fox asked in 1962 whether non-invertible knots exist; Trotter found the first examples in 1963, and Kawauchi proved in 1979 that 8₁₇ is not invertible (Bonahon and Siebenmann gave an independent proof). 8₁₇ is amphichiral in a special way: its mirror image is the same knot with the direction reversed.

What the animation shows. The knot is drawn as a closed braid on three strands, σ₁²σ₂⁻¹σ₁σ₂⁻¹σ₁σ₂⁻², with arrows giving its direction. It is reflected; the reflection is labelled in the film as an operation, not a motion. The mirror image is then moved, without cutting, back onto the knot: it is turned half a turn and its closing strands are swung around. It is back where it started, but every arrow is reversed (teal marks the original direction). Reversing the arrows again is only relabelling, also labelled in the film: mirror image plus reversal brings 8₁₇ back to itself. No motion reverses the arrows alone. Every step of the motion is checked: no strand passes through another.

H. F. Trotter, Non-invertible knots exist, Topology 2 (1963); A. Kawauchi, Proc. Japan Acad. Ser. A 55 (1979), 399–402.
