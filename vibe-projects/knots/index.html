Knots

Played with AI (Grok bot) to learn something about knots. A knot is a closed loop in space. Two knots are the same if one can be moved onto the other without cutting it and without passing it through itself. On a drawing, every such motion is a sequence of three local Reidemeister moves.

Here are five examples where the answer is hard to guess from the picture. Click a knot to open its animation (each opens on its own page).

  1. The Perko pair — two entries in the knot tables, 10₁₆₁ and 10₁₆₂, are the same knot.
  2. The Culprit — a drawing of the unknot that must get more tangled before it can come apart.
  3. The figure-eight knot — it can be moved onto its own mirror image.
  4. The Conway knot and the Kinoshita–Terasaka knot — mutants that share many invariants but are different knots.
  5. 8₁₇ — the smallest knot that cannot be turned around: no motion takes it onto itself with its direction reversed.
