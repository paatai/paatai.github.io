The Perko pair

Watch the video (MP4, 6 MB)

Silent animation

Why it is remarkable. For about 75 years the knot tables listed as two different knots what is really one knot. C. N. Little’s tables of 10-crossing knots (1899–1900) contain both diagrams, and they were carried over into later tables, where they became 10₁₆₁ and 10₁₆₂. In 1973 Kenneth Perko, a lawyer who had studied knot theory as a Princeton undergraduate, found how to move one diagram onto the mirror image of the other, and published it in 1974. Both diagrams have 10 crossings, the fewest possible for this knot, yet their writhes (the signed count of crossings) are different, −8 and −10. So the pair also refuted Little’s claim that all diagrams of a knot with the fewest crossings have the same writhe. The underlying problem: decide whether two drawings show the same knot.

What the animation shows. On the left is 10₁₆₁, on the right the mirror image of 10₁₆₂. The left diagram is moved in nine Reidemeister moves: two type III moves (writhe unchanged), a type I move that twists in a negative kink (11 crossings, writhe −9), five more type III moves, and a type I move that untwists the only positive crossing (10 crossings, writhe −10). The result is the mirror image of 10₁₆₂, crossing for crossing. A counter shows the number of crossings and the writhe. Every step between frames is checked: no strand passes through another.

C. N. Little, Trans. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh 39 (1900); K. A. Perko, Jr., On the classification of knots, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 45 (1974), 262–266.
